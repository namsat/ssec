Chapter 08

The p–n junction

Joining p-type and n-type material creates a barrier whose height is controlled by the applied voltage. The exponential dependence of minority-carrier injection on that barrier gives the diode its rectifying characteristic, and the same structure detects light, converts sunlight to power and emits photons.

Notation: $V_T=k_BT/q$ (25.85 mV at 300 K), $V$ is the voltage of the p side relative to the n side, $x=0$ is the metallurgical junction, and the depletion region extends from $-x_p$ to $x_n$.

1. The junction in equilibrium

When the two sides are joined, electrons diffuse from n to p and holes from p to n, leaving behind ionized donors and acceptors. This space charge creates a field that opposes further diffusion. In equilibrium drift and diffusion cancel for each carrier separately and $E_F$ is flat.

Built-in potential
$$V_{bi}=V_T\ln\frac{N_AN_D}{n_i^2}.$$
Two derivations

From the band diagram. Far from the junction $E_F-E_i=k_BT\ln(N_D/n_i)$ on the n side and $E_i-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_A/n_i)$ on the p side. With $E_F$ flat, $E_i$ must drop by the sum of the two across the junction: $qV_{bi}=k_BT\ln(N_AN_D/n_i^2)$.

From zero current. $J_p=q\mu_ppE-qD_p\frac{\dd p}{\dd x}=0$ gives $E=V_T\frac1p\frac{\dd p}{\dd x}$. Integrating $-E$ across the junction: $V_{bi}=V_T\ln\frac{p_p}{p_n}=V_T\ln\frac{N_A}{n_i^2/N_D}$.

∎

Depletion approximation. Assume the region $-x_p < x < x_n$ is completely free of carriers and the rest is neutral. Poisson's equation $\frac{\dd E}{\dd x}=\rho/\varepsilon$ with $\rho=-qN_A$ and $+qN_D$ gives a triangular field and a parabolic potential:

$$N_Ax_p=N_Dx_n,\qquad E_{\max}=\frac{qN_Dx_n}{\varepsilon}=\frac{2(V_{bi}-V)}{W},\qquad W=x_n+x_p=\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon}{q}\Big(\frac{1}{N_A}+\frac{1}{N_D}\Big)(V_{bi}-V)} .$$
Derivation

Integrate once with $E=0$ at the edges: $E(x)=-\frac{qN_A}{\varepsilon}(x+x_p)$ for $-x_p\le x\le0$ and $E(x)=-\frac{qN_D}{\varepsilon}(x_n-x)$ for $0\le x\le x_n$. Continuity at $x=0$ gives $N_Ax_p=N_Dx_n$ (overall neutrality). The potential drop is the area of the triangle: $V_{bi}-V=\frac12|E_{\max}|W$. Using $x_n=WN_A/(N_A+N_D)$ in $|E_{\max}|=qN_Dx_n/\varepsilon$ and solving for $W$ yields the result. The depletion region extends mostly into the lightly doped side.

∎

2. Applied voltage, depletion charge and capacitance

With bias the barrier becomes $V_{bi}-V$. The charge stored on each side of the depletion region is $Q_j=qN_Dx_nA$, and a small change of voltage adds or removes charge at the edges of the region, exactly like a parallel-plate capacitor of thickness $W$:

$$C_j=\Big|\frac{\dd Q_j}{\dd V}\Big|=\frac{\varepsilon A}{W(V)}=\frac{C_{j0}}{\sqrt{1-V/V_{bi}}},\qquad C_{j0}=A\sqrt{\frac{q\varepsilon}{2V_{bi}}\frac{N_AN_D}{N_A+N_D}} .$$

For a linearly graded junction the exponent is $1/3$ instead of $1/2$; in general $C_j=C_{j0}(1-V/V_{bi})^{-m}$. The voltage-dependent capacitance is used in varactors.

Measuring the built-in potential and the doping from C–V
$$\frac{1}{C_j^2}=\frac{2}{q\varepsilon A^2N_{eff}}\,(V_{bi}-V),\qquad N_{eff}=\frac{N_AN_D}{N_A+N_D}\approx N_{\text{lighter side}} .$$

A plot of $1/C^2$ against reverse voltage is a straight line. Its intercept with the voltage axis gives $V_{bi}$ and its slope gives the doping of the lightly doped side. For non-uniform doping the local slope gives the profile: $N(W)=-\dfrac{2}{q\varepsilon A^2}\Big[\dfrac{\dd(1/C^2)}{\dd V}\Big]^{-1}$ at depth $W=\varepsilon A/C$.

3. Quasi-Fermi levels and the law of the junction

Under bias the system is out of equilibrium. In the depletion region the drift and diffusion currents of each carrier are each much larger than their difference, so each carrier type remains in near-equilibrium with its own side: the quasi-Fermi levels $E_{Fn}$ and $E_{Fp}$ are essentially flat across the depletion region and separated by $qV$.

Law of the junction
$$pn=n_i^2\,e^{V/V_T}\ \ \text{throughout the depletion region}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_n(x_n)=p_{n0}\,e^{V/V_T},\qquad n_p(-x_p)=n_{p0}\,e^{V/V_T}.$$
Proof

From Chapter 07, $np=n_i^2e^{(E_{Fn}-E_{Fp})/k_BT}$. With $E_{Fn}-E_{Fp}=qV$ in the depletion region the first relation follows. At the edge $x=x_n$, low-level injection means the majority concentration is unchanged, $n_n\simeq N_D$, so $p_n(x_n)=\frac{n_i^2}{N_D}e^{V/V_T}=p_{n0}e^{V/V_T}$, and similarly on the p side.

Why the quasi-Fermi levels are flat: $J_n=\mu_nn\,\dd E_{Fn}/\dd x$. In the depletion region $n$ is still large compared with the minority concentration it will become, and $W$ is short, so the drop $\Delta E_{Fn}=\int J_n/(\mu_nn)\,\dd x$ is a small fraction of $k_BT$ for the currents that flow.

∎

Forward bias raises the minority concentration at the edges exponentially (injection); reverse bias drives it to zero (extraction). These boundary values are all that the depletion region contributes to the ideal diode.

4. Currents in the junction and the ideal diode

Assumptions: depletion approximation, low-level injection, no generation or recombination in the depletion region, quasi-neutral regions long compared with the diffusion lengths. In each neutral region the minority carriers obey the diffusion equation of Chapter 07:

$$\Delta p_n(x)=p_{n0}\big(e^{V/V_T}-1\big)e^{-(x-x_n)/L_p},\qquad\Delta n_p(x)=n_{p0}\big(e^{V/V_T}-1\big)e^{(x+x_p)/L_n}.$$
Shockley diode equation
$$I=I_S\big(e^{V/V_T}-1\big),\qquad I_S=qAn_i^2\Big(\frac{D_p}{L_pN_D}+\frac{D_n}{L_nN_A}\Big).$$
Derivation

The minority currents at the depletion edges are pure diffusion currents: $J_p(x_n)=-qD_p\frac{\dd\Delta p_n}{\dd x}\big|_{x_n}=\frac{qD_pp_{n0}}{L_p}(e^{V/V_T}-1)$, and $J_n(-x_p)=\frac{qD_nn_{p0}}{L_n}(e^{V/V_T}-1)$. With no generation or recombination in the depletion region each current is constant across it, so the total current, which is the same at every $x$, is $J=J_p(x_n)+J_n(-x_p)$. Using $p_{n0}=n_i^2/N_D$ and $n_{p0}=n_i^2/N_A$ gives $I_S$.

For neutral regions of length $W_n,W_p$ shorter than the diffusion lengths (short diode), replace $L_p\to W_n$ and $L_n\to W_p$.

∎

Distribution of the currents. Far from the junction all the current is carried by majority carriers drifting in a small field. Approaching the junction, part of that majority current feeds recombination with the injected minority carriers, and is replaced by minority diffusion current. In a p⁺n diode almost all the current crossing the junction is hole current, since $J_p/J_n\approx N_A/N_D$ (the principle of emitter efficiency in the BJT). In reverse bias the same distribution holds with opposite sign: the current $-I_S$ is carried by minority carriers generated thermally within a diffusion length of the junction and swept across by the field.

5. The static characteristic of real diodes

  • Generation–recombination in the depletion region. SRH recombination peaks where $n\approx p$ and adds $I_{rec}\approx\dfrac{qAn_iW}{2\tau_0}\,e^{V/2V_T}$; in reverse bias the generation current $I_{gen}=qAn_iW/2\tau_0$ grows with $\sqrt{V_{bi}-V}$ and usually exceeds $I_S$ in silicon. An empirical ideality factor $1\le\eta\le2$ covers both: $I=I_S(e^{V/\eta V_T}-1)$.
  • High-level injection. When injected minority carriers reach the majority density, $pn=n_i^2e^{V/V_T}$ with $p\approx n$ gives $I\propto e^{V/2V_T}$.
  • Series resistance. The neutral regions and contacts drop $IR_S$: $V=\eta V_T\ln(I/I_S+1)+IR_S$. On a semilog plot the curve bends away from the straight line at high current, by exactly $IR_S$.
  • Temperature. $I_S\propto n_i^2\propto T^3e^{-E_g/k_BT}$ roughly doubles every 10 K (the measured reverse current, dominated by generation, about every 10–12 K). At fixed current, $\dd V/\dd T\approx-2$ mV/K, used in temperature sensors.
Turn-on (cut-in) voltage $V_\gamma$

The exponential has no true threshold, but on a linear current scale the diode appears to switch on at a voltage that depends logarithmically on the current scale of interest: $V_\gamma\approx\eta V_T\ln(I_{ref}/I_S)$. For silicon at milliampere currents $V_\gamma\approx0.6$–$0.7$ V; for Ge about 0.3 V; for Schottky diodes 0.2–0.4 V; for GaAs about 1.1 V; for a blue LED about 2.7 V. Each decade of current costs only $\eta\cdot60$ mV, which justifies the piecewise-linear model: open circuit below $V_\gamma$, a source $V_\gamma$ in series with a small resistance above.

6. Electrical breakdown of the junction

At a sufficiently large reverse voltage the current rises abruptly. The process is not destructive if the current is limited externally. Two mechanisms:

Avalanche multiplication

A carrier accelerated by the field gains enough energy (about $1.5E_g$) to create an electron–hole pair by impact ionization; the new carriers do the same. Breakdown occurs when the ionization integral reaches one, $\int_0^W\alpha(E)\,\dd x=1$, i.e. at a critical field $E_{crit}\approx3$–$6\times10^5$ V/cm in Si:

$$V_{BR}\simeq\frac{\varepsilon E_{crit}^2}{2qN}.$$

Dominant for $V_{BR}\gtrsim6E_g/q$ (about 7 V in Si). Positive temperature coefficient: phonon scattering shortens the mean free path.

Zener (tunnelling) breakdown

In heavily doped junctions the depletion region is so thin (< 10 nm) that valence electrons on the p side tunnel through the gap into the conduction band of the n side. With a triangular barrier of height $E_g$ and width $E_g/qE$ (WKB, Chapter 02):

$$T\approx\exp\Big(-\frac{4\sqrt{2m^*}E_g^{3/2}}{3q\hbar E}\Big).$$

Dominant for $V_{BR}\lesssim4E_g/q$ (about 5 V in Si). Negative temperature coefficient: $E_g$ shrinks with $T$.

Zener diodes near 5–6 V combine both mechanisms and have nearly zero temperature coefficient, which is why voltage references use that range. In real planar junctions breakdown occurs first at the curved edges, where the field is enhanced; guard rings and field plates are used to approach the plane-junction value. Punch-through is a third limit: the depletion region reaches the opposite contact before the critical field is attained.

7. Charge-control model, diffusion capacitance and circuit models

In forward bias excess minority charge is stored in the neutral regions. For a p⁺n diode, $Q_p=qA\int\Delta p_n\,\dd x=qAL_pp_{n0}(e^{V/V_T}-1)$. Integrating the continuity equation over the neutral region gives the charge-control equation:

$$i(t)=\frac{Q_p}{\tau_p}+\frac{\dd Q_p}{\dd t}\qquad\Big(\text{steady state: }I=\frac{Q_p}{\tau_p}\Big).$$

The current supplies the charge lost to recombination plus any change of stored charge. For a short diode the lifetime is replaced by the transit time $\tau_T=W_n^2/2D_p$. The stored charge varies with voltage, which is a capacitance:

$$C_d=\frac{\dd Q_p}{\dd V}=\frac{\tau_TI}{V_T}\quad\text{(diffusion capacitance)},\qquad r_d=\Big(\frac{\dd I}{\dd V}\Big)^{-1}=\frac{\eta V_T}{I}\quad\text{(dynamic resistance)}.$$
Large-signal model

A nonlinear current source $I_S(e^{v/\eta V_T}-1)$ in parallel with two nonlinear charge-storage elements, $Q_j(v)$ (depletion) and $Q_d=\tau_Ti_D$ (diffusion), plus $R_S$ in series. This is the diode model of SPICE (parameters IS, N, RS, CJO, VJ, M, TT, BV).

Small-signal model

Linearizing around the bias point $(V_Q,I_Q)$: a conductance $g_d=I_Q/\eta V_T$ in parallel with $C_j(V_Q)+C_d(I_Q)$, plus $R_S$. In forward bias $C_d\propto I$ dominates; in reverse bias only $C_j$ remains and $g_d\approx0$. Since $r_dC_d=\tau_T$, the diode response rolls off at $f\approx1/2\pi\tau_T$.

Switching. When a forward-biased diode is suddenly reverse-driven, the junction voltage cannot change until the stored charge is removed: during the storage time the diode conducts a large reverse current $I_R$. Solving the charge-control equation with $i=-I_R$:

$$t_s=\tau_p\ln\Big(1+\frac{I_F}{I_R}\Big).$$

Fast diodes reduce the lifetime (gold or platinum doping, irradiation) or avoid minority storage altogether (Schottky diodes).

8. Light and the junction: photodiode, solar cell, LED

A photon with $h\nu>E_g$ creates an electron–hole pair. Pairs generated in the depletion region, or within a diffusion length of it, are separated by the field: electrons go to the n side and holes to the p side. This adds a reverse current proportional to the photon flux:

$$I=I_S\big(e^{V/\eta V_T}-1\big)-I_{ph},\qquad I_{ph}=qA\,G\,(W+L_n+L_p)=\eta_{ext}\,\frac{q\lambda}{hc}\,P_{opt}.$$
