Chapter 02

One-dimensional quantum systems

Five exactly solvable problems contain most of the physics used later: confinement quantizes energy, barriers can be tunnelled through, the harmonic oscillator has equally spaced levels, and a periodic potential turns levels into bands separated by gaps.

Throughout, $\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m_0}=0.0381\ \text{eV·nm}^2$, so an electron of effective mass $m^*$ with wavevector $k$ (in nm⁻¹) has kinetic energy $0.0381\,k^2/(m^*/m_0)$ eV.

1. The infinite well

Inside $0 < x < L$ the potential vanishes and $\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0$. The solutions of $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''=E\psi$ that satisfy the boundary conditions are standing waves:

$$\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L},\qquad E_n=\frac{\hbar^2\pi^2n^2}{2mL^2}=\frac{0.376\ \text{eV}}{(m/m_0)\,(L/\text{nm})^2}\,n^2,\qquad n=1,2,\dots$$

Three features are general: the ground-state energy is not zero (a consequence of $\Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\hbar/2$), the $n$-th state has $n-1$ nodes, and eigenfunctions are orthonormal, $\int\psi_m\psi_n\,\dd x=\delta_{mn}$. A superposition of two states is not stationary: its density oscillates at the Bohr frequency $\omega_{mn}=(E_m-E_n)/\hbar$.

2. The finite well

For a well of depth $V_0$ and width $a$ the wavefunction leaks into the barriers as $e^{-\kappa|x|}$ with $\kappa=\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar$. Matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x=\pm a/2$ gives, with $k=\sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$,

$$k\tan\frac{ka}{2}=\kappa\ \ (\text{even}),\qquad -k\cot\frac{ka}{2}=\kappa\ \ (\text{odd}),\qquad k^2+\kappa^2=\frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}.$$

These transcendental equations are solved graphically or numerically. The number of bound states is $N=\big\lceil\sqrt{2mV_0a^2}/(\pi\hbar)\big\rceil$; there is always at least one. Levels lie below those of the infinite well because the wavefunction is less confined.

3. Potential step, barrier and tunnelling

A particle of energy $E$ incident on a region of potential $V_0$ is described by plane waves, $\psi=e^{ikx}+r\,e^{-ikx}$ on the left and $t\,e^{ik'x}$ on the right. Reflection and transmission probabilities are ratios of probability currents: $R=|r|^2$, $T=\frac{k'}{k}|t|^2$, $R+T=1$.

Step ($E>V_0$): $R=\Big(\dfrac{k-k'}{k+k'}\Big)^2$. A quantum particle is partly reflected even when it has enough energy to pass. For $E < V_0$, $R=1$ but the wave penetrates a distance $1/\kappa$.

Rectangular barrier, $E < V_0$
$$T=\left[1+\frac{V_0^2\sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0-E)}\right]^{-1}\ \xrightarrow{\ \kappa a\gg1\ }\ \frac{16E(V_0-E)}{V_0^2}\,e^{-2\kappa a},\qquad\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}.$$

For $E>V_0$ replace $\sinh\kappa a\to\sin k'a$: $T=1$ whenever $k'a=n\pi$ (transmission resonances).

Derivation by matching

Write $\psi_I=e^{ikx}+re^{-ikx}$, $\psi_{II}=Ae^{\kappa x}+Be^{-\kappa x}$, $\psi_{III}=te^{ikx}$. Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x=0$ and $x=a$ gives four linear equations. Eliminating $A$, $B$ and $r$:

$$t\,e^{ika}=\Big[\cosh\kappa a-\frac{i}{2}\Big(\frac k\kappa-\frac\kappa k\Big)\sinh\kappa a\Big]^{-1}.$$

Hence $1/T=\cosh^2\kappa a+\frac14\big(\frac k\kappa-\frac\kappa k\big)^2\sinh^2\kappa a=1+\frac14\big(\frac k\kappa+\frac\kappa k\big)^2\sinh^2\kappa a$, and $(k/\kappa+\kappa/k)^2=V_0^2/[E(V_0-E)]$.

∎

For a slowly varying barrier the WKB approximation generalizes the exponential: $T\approx\exp\!\big[-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\kappa(x)\,\dd x\big]$. Applied to a triangular barrier it gives the Fowler–Nordheim current used to program flash memories, and applied to the gap of a reverse-biased junction it gives Zener tunnelling.

4. The harmonic oscillator and ladder operators

$\hat H=\dfrac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\dfrac12m\omega^2\hat x^2$ describes any system near a stable equilibrium: molecular vibrations, lattice modes (phonons) and each mode of the electromagnetic field. Define the dimensionless operators

$$\hat a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x+\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\Big),\qquad\hat a^\dagger=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\Big(\hat x-\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}\Big),\qquad[\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1 .$$
Spectrum
$$\hat H=\hbar\omega\big(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+\tfrac12\big),\qquad E_n=\hbar\omega\big(n+\tfrac12\big),\quad n=0,1,2,\dots$$ $$\hat a\ket n=\sqrt n\,\ket{n-1},\qquad\hat a^\dagger\ket n=\sqrt{n+1}\,\ket{n+1},\qquad\ket n=\frac{(\hat a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket0 .$$
Algebraic proof

From $[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$: $\hat a^\dagger\hat a=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{2m\hbar\omega}+\frac{i}{2\hbar}[\hat x,\hat p]=\frac{\hat H}{\hbar\omega}-\frac12$. Let $\hat N=\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ with $\hat N\ket\nu=\nu\ket\nu$.

Step 1. $\nu=\bra\nu\hat a^\dagger\hat a\ket\nu=\|\hat a\ket\nu\|^2\ge0$.

Step 2. $[\hat N,\hat a]=-\hat a$ and $[\hat N,\hat a^\dagger]=\hat a^\dagger$, so $\hat N(\hat a\ket\nu)=(\nu-1)\hat a\ket\nu$ and $\hat N(\hat a^\dagger\ket\nu)=(\nu+1)\hat a^\dagger\ket\nu$: $\hat a$ lowers and $\hat a^\dagger$ raises the eigenvalue by one.

Step 3. Repeated lowering would reach negative eigenvalues, contradicting step 1, unless the sequence terminates: $\hat a\ket0=0$. This requires $\nu$ to be a non-negative integer $n$.

Step 4. Norms: $\|\hat a\ket n\|^2=n$ and $\|\hat a^\dagger\ket n\|^2=\bra n(\hat N+1)\ket n=n+1$ fix the factors $\sqrt n$ and $\sqrt{n+1}$.

In position space $\hat a\psi_0=0$ reads $\big(x+\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{\dd}{\dd x}\big)\psi_0=0$, whose solution is the Gaussian $\psi_0=(m\omega/\pi\hbar)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar}$. Applying $\hat a^\dagger$ generates the Hermite functions $\psi_n(\xi)=(2^nn!\sqrt\pi)^{-1/2}H_n(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}$, $\xi=x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$.

∎

Expectation values follow without integrals: $\hat x=\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger)$ gives $\expval{x}=0$, $\expval{x^2}=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}(n+\frac12)$, hence $\Delta x\,\Delta p=(n+\frac12)\hbar$: the ground state saturates the uncertainty bound.

5. Coherent states and the semiclassical picture

Energy eigenstates have $\expval{x}=0$ at all times and do not resemble a swinging mass. The states that do are the eigenstates of the annihilation operator:

$$\hat a\ket\alpha=\alpha\ket\alpha,\qquad\ket\alpha=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket n,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb C .$$
  • The number distribution is Poissonian: $P(n)=e^{-|\alpha|^2}|\alpha|^{2n}/n!$, with $\expval n=|\alpha|^2$ and $\Delta n=|\alpha|$.
  • Time evolution keeps the state coherent: $\ket{\alpha(t)}=e^{-i\omega t/2}\ket{\alpha e^{-i\omega t}}$.
  • $\expval{x}(t)=\sqrt{2\hbar/m\omega}\,|\alpha|\cos(\omega t-\varphi)$: the mean follows the classical trajectory exactly.
  • The packet is a displaced ground state, so $\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar/2$ at all times and it does not spread.
Proof of the expansion and of the time evolution

Write $\ket\alpha=\sum_nc_n\ket n$. The eigenvalue equation gives $c_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}=\alpha c_n$, so $c_n=c_0\alpha^n/\sqrt{n!}$; normalization fixes $|c_0|^2=e^{-|\alpha|^2}$. Evolving each component with $e^{-i(n+1/2)\omega t}$:

$$\ket{\alpha(t)}=e^{-i\omega t/2}e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n\frac{(\alpha e^{-i\omega t})^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket n=e^{-i\omega t/2}\ket{\alpha e^{-i\omega t}} .$$

∎

Laser light is a coherent state of a field mode, with Poissonian photon statistics. The relative fluctuation $\Delta n/\expval n=1/|\alpha|$ vanishes for large amplitude, which is the classical limit.

6. An infinite array of wells: the Kronig–Penney model

An electron in a crystal moves in a potential with the period $a$ of the lattice, $V(x+a)=V(x)$. Bloch's theorem (proved in Crystals) states that eigenfunctions can be chosen as

$$\psi_k(x)=e^{ikx}u_k(x),\qquad u_k(x+a)=u_k(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\psi_k(x+a)=e^{ika}\psi_k(x).$$

Take delta barriers of strength $\lambda$ at $x=na$: $V(x)=\lambda\sum_n\delta(x-na)$. Between barriers $\psi=A\sin\alpha x+B\cos\alpha x$ with $E=\hbar^2\alpha^2/2m$.

Kronig–Penney dispersion relation
$$\cos ka=\cos\alpha a+P\,\frac{\sin\alpha a}{\alpha a},\qquad P=\frac{m\lambda a}{\hbar^2}.$$
Derivation

In the cell $0 < x < a$: $\psi=A\sin\alpha x+B\cos\alpha x$. By Bloch's theorem the function in the cell $-a < x < 0$ is $\psi(x)=e^{-ika}[A\sin\alpha(x+a)+B\cos\alpha(x+a)]$.

Continuity at $x=0$: $B=e^{-ika}(A\sin\alpha a+B\cos\alpha a)$.

The delta function makes $\psi'$ jump: integrating the Schrödinger equation across $x=0$, $\psi'(0^+)-\psi'(0^-)=\frac{2m\lambda}{\hbar^2}\psi(0)$, i.e. $\alpha A-e^{-ika}\alpha(A\cos\alpha a-B\sin\alpha a)=\frac{2m\lambda}{\hbar^2}B$.

Solving the first equation for $A$ and substituting in the second, then multiplying by $e^{ika}$ and simplifying with $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$, gives $\cos ka=\cos\alpha a+\frac{m\lambda}{\hbar^2\alpha}\sin\alpha a$.

∎

The left side is bounded by $\pm1$, the right side is not. Energies for which $|f(\alpha a)|>1$ admit no Bloch solution: these are the forbidden gaps. Allowed energies form bands. Limits: $P\to0$ recovers the free electron $E=\hbar^2k^2/2m$; $P\to\infty$ gives the discrete levels of isolated wells, $\alpha a=n\pi$. Gaps open at $k=n\pi/a$, where the electron wavelength satisfies the Bragg condition and the eigenstates are standing waves.

Electrons in crystalline solids: what the model tells us
  • $N$ cells give $N$ allowed $k$ values per band, so each band holds $2N$ electrons (spin). A crystal with an odd number of electrons per cell must be a metal; with an even number it can be an insulator or a semiconductor if a gap separates filled from empty bands.
  • The band velocity is $v=\frac1\hbar\frac{\dd E}{\dd k}$ and vanishes at zone boundaries.
  • Curvature defines the effective mass $1/m^*=\frac{1}{\hbar^2}\frac{\dd^2E}{\dd k^2}$, negative near the top of a band, which leads to the concept of holes.
